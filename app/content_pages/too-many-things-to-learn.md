H1: Too many things to learn? How to organize it

Curiosity does not come with a limit. Next to the course you are halfway through there is a
playlist you saved, a book, and a language you meant to start. The problem is rarely time. It
is that nothing has a place, so everything feels unfinished at once.

## 1. Write down the goals, not the videos

A goal is what you want to be able to do: "build a small web app", "hold a conversation in
Italian", "understand statistics for my thesis". Videos, PDFs and notes are material that
serves a goal. Sort material under the goal it belongs to, and material without a goal is
the first candidate to drop.

## 2. Limit what is active

Pick two or three goals for the next few weeks. The rest wait in a list. This is not giving up.
It is deciding the order.

## 3. Rate importance honestly

Not everything deserves the same number of reviews. A concept you will use daily should come
back often. Background trivia can come back rarely or not at all.

## 4. Review in short, scheduled sessions

Ten minutes a day of quizzing yourself beats a two-hour catch-up on the weekend. Reviews that
are spaced out, a day, then a few days, then a week, fit around other things and tire you less.

## 5. Accept that you will forget some of it

Forgetting is how memory works. A system that expects it, and lets old unused material drop out
of the way, is easier to live with than a list that only grows.
