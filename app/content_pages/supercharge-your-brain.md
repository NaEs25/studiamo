H1: Supercharge your brain with better breaks

More hours are not always the way to learn more or think better. What you do between them
matters too. These four kinds of breaks have research behind them. Each one does a different
job, and none of them costs anything.

## 1. Rest quietly after you learn something

Sit with your eyes closed for ten minutes after a lesson, before starting the next one. In one
study, a story followed by rest was remembered as well a week later as another story was after
half an hour. Pooled studies show a smaller but real benefit.

**Takes:** 10 minutes. **Good for:** remembering what you just learned.

[Read more: Rest for ten minutes after you learn something](/wakeful-rest-after-learning)

## 2. Stuck? Step away and do something easy

When you hit a wall on a problem, a short break spent on something easy, like a walk or the
dishes, can bring better ideas than pushing on. In one experiment, sitting quietly or
switching to hard mental work did not help. The easy task did.

**Takes:** 10 to 15 minutes. **Good for:** creative problems and finding a new angle.

[Read more: Stuck on a problem? Take a break and do something easy](/incubation-break-when-stuck)

## 3. Look away from the screen, and walk outside

Short breaks leave you less tired. Looking into the distance is cheap, though the evidence for
the 20-20-20 rule is mixed. Walks through green places improved attention more than walks
along city streets.

**Takes:** 20 seconds to 20 minutes. **Good for:** tired eyes and a tired mind.

[Read more: Screen breaks that help: look away, step away, walk outside](/screen-breaks-and-walks)

## 4. Rest or nap in the afternoon

A guided body scan like yoga nidra, also sold as NSDR, brings small gains in stress and sleep.
The famous "65 percent more dopamine" comes from a study of eight people. For an afternoon
slump, a 10 to 20 minute nap has the clearest evidence.

**Takes:** 10 to 20 minutes. **Good for:** the afternoon slump.

[Read more: NSDR and yoga nidra: what a 20-minute afternoon rest can do](/nsdr-yoga-nidra)

## At a glance

| Break | Time | Best for | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quiet rest after learning | 10 min | Remembering new material | Moderate. Pooled studies support it; the effect is smaller than first reported. |
| Easy task when stuck | 10–15 min | Creative problems | Moderate. A large review supports it; one popular explanation did not hold up. |
| Screen breaks and walks | 20 s–20 min | Fatigue, eye strain, attention | Moderate for short breaks and nature walks; mixed for the 20-20-20 rule. |
| Yoga nidra, NSDR or a nap | 10–20 min | Afternoon slump | Small effects for yoga nidra; better evidence for short naps. |

## Claims that did not hold up

Some popular claims about rest fall apart when you look up the studies behind them:

- **"Panoramic vision calms your nervous system in 90 seconds."** No peer-reviewed study shows
  this.
- **"NSDR restores your dopamine."** This rests on one study of eight people that measured
  dopamine during the session, not after.
- **Exact figures like "30 percent better recall."** Larger studies show smaller effects than
  the early ones.
