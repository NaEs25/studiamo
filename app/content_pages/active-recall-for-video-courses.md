H1: Active recall for video courses

Video courses are built for watching. Active recall is the opposite: closing the material and
pulling it out of memory. Combining them takes a few deliberate habits.

## What active recall means

Active recall is trying to retrieve an answer before looking at it. Flashcards, practice
questions, and explaining a concept from memory are all forms of it. The effort of retrieving
is what builds the memory, which cognitive psychologists call the testing effect.

## How to apply it to a course

**Pause at the end of a section, not in the middle.** When a section ends, close the tab and
ask: what were the three main ideas, and how do they connect? Say or write the answer first.

**Ask questions that need more than a yes or no.** "Why does this approach fail on large
inputs?" is better than "Did the instructor mention caching?"

**Quiz early, then again later.** One review right after watching is useful. The larger gain
comes from returning after a day, then a few days, then a week or more. The gaps feel
uncomfortable because you will have forgotten some of it. That is the point.

**Mix topics.** Reviewing different lessons in the same session is harder than reviewing one at
a time, and it gives better long-term retention than studying blocks in isolation.

## What to skip

Re-watching the whole lecture and highlighting transcripts feel productive and rate low in the
research (Dunlosky et al., 2013).
