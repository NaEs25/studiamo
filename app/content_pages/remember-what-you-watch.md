H1: How to remember what you learn from YouTube videos

You watch a good tutorial, it all makes sense, and a week later you can barely describe it.
That is not a personal failing. Watching feels like learning because the material is clear
while it is in front of you. Clear is not the same as remembered.

## Why it fades

Hermann Ebbinghaus measured this in the 1880s: without review, memory for new material drops
quickly at first and then levels off. A video gives you no reason to review. You press play,
nod along, and move to the next one.

## What works instead

Two habits have strong support in the research on learning.

**1. Quiz yourself instead of rewatching.** In a well-known 2006 study, Roediger and Karpicke
had students read a passage and then either re-read it or take a test on it. Re-reading felt
better and scored better on a test five minutes later. A week later the tested group remembered
more. Pulling an answer out of memory is what strengthens it.

**2. Space the reviews.** Reviewing the same thing a day later, then a few days later, then
weeks later beats cramming it into one sitting. Reviews at growing intervals catch the memory
just as it starts to fade.

Dunlosky and colleagues (2013) compared ten common study techniques. Practice testing and
spaced practice rated highest for usefulness. Highlighting and re-reading rated lowest.

## A routine you can use today

1. Watch the video once, without pausing every ten seconds to copy notes.
2. Close it. Write down everything you remember, or answer questions about it.
3. Check what you missed. Those gaps are what to review.
4. Come back to the same questions a day later, then a few days later, then about a week later.

The hard part is step 2 and 4: someone has to write the questions and remember to ask them.
