H1: Tutorial fatigue: why you keep watching and keep forgetting

You finish a tutorial and feel good. The next day you open the editor, or the textbook, and
the knowledge is not there. So you watch another tutorial. This loop is common enough that
people call it tutorial hell, and it is tiring in a specific way: you put in the hours and
have little to show for them.

This is about learning overload, not a medical condition. If you are exhausted in a way that
sleep does not fix, that is a question for a doctor, not a study method.

## Why more watching does not help

Watching is passive. The material is clear while the instructor explains it, which feels like
understanding. Memory forms when you pull information back out, and a video never asks you to.
Re-reading and re-watching feel productive and rated among the least useful techniques in a
2013 review of ten study methods (Dunlosky et al.). Practice testing and spaced practice rated
among the most useful.

## Why it feels so draining

- Every new video adds to a pile you will never finish reviewing.
- Forgetting the last one makes you feel behind, so you start another.
- Nothing ever gets marked as done, so the effort has no end point.

## Smaller loops

1. **Watch less, quiz more.** After a video, close it and answer a few questions from memory
   before starting the next one.
2. **Review on a schedule.** A day, a few days, a week. Short sessions.
3. **Let some things go.** You do not need to remember everything you watched. Pick what you
   will use and ignore the rest on purpose.
