H1: An Anki alternative for learning from YouTube

Anki is a strong spaced repetition tool, free and open source, with a huge deck library and
years of use by medical and language students. If you enjoy building your own cards, it is
hard to beat. This page is for the case where the card-making is what stops you.

## Where the friction is

Learning from video usually looks like: watch a lecture, intend to make cards, never make
them. Making good cards means rewatching, deciding what matters, and phrasing each question.
For a 40-minute video that can take longer than the video.

## How Studiamo differs

| | Anki | Studiamo |
|---|---|---|
| Cards come from | You write them (or import a shared deck) | Generated from a YouTube link, PDF, or your notes |
| Scheduling | Spaced repetition, highly configurable | Stage-based spaced repetition, reminders by push or email |
| Question types | Whatever you design | Multiple choice early, written recall as material matures |
| Best for | Dense, long-term decks you curate | Turning videos and documents into review quickly |

## Honest limits

Generated questions are not always perfect, and you can edit them. If you need precise
control over every card, image occlusion, or add-ons, Anki is the better tool. Many people use
both: Anki for a core deck, Studiamo for the lectures they would otherwise never review.

## Try the routine either way

Whatever you use, the method is the same: quiz yourself from memory and space the reviews.
